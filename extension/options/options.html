Subtext settings

Scanning runs in your browser. The settings below describe which lookups contact a site or service. Investigate and Deep analyze send page text to your selected AI provider using your key. Single-pass analysis can also send chart images.

Context from public data

"Show context" in the report works out what the page is about, then asks free public sources that can answer a question about that subject: IRS filings for a nonprofit, SEC filings for a public company, an official statistic the page names, news coverage of the story. No AI key is used. It makes at most 18 requests. Each request sends only an identifier, a name or a few headline words, never the page address or its text, and no Referer. "What was looked up" in the report lists every request. When no record or statistic fits the page, it shows no chart.

On pages about political money, Show context can add what a committee linked to the organization reported to the Federal Election Commission. The FEC's API needs a free key from api.data.gov. The key is stored unencrypted in this browser profile and is sent only to api.open.fec.gov. Without a key this one source is skipped.

AI: Investigate (optional)

Both keys are stored unencrypted in this browser profile, in Subtext's own storage, which the parts of Subtext that run inside web pages cannot read. Only the selected provider's key is used. You can get a key with a spending limit from . Leave the key blank to turn AI analysis off. AI requests run only when you click Investigate, Deep analyze, or Analyze selected evidence in your casebook.

An inquiry uses three AI requests (plan, draft, review), plus one web-search request when the background search below is on and the planner judges it useful, and reads up to two public sources. A step whose answer is cut off is retried once. Excerpts from retrieved sources go to your AI provider. To find a source it may search public records (the SEC at sec.gov and efts.sec.gov, Europe PMC at ebi.ac.uk, PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, doi.org) with names or identifiers taken from the page, and it may read the site's own disclosure and terms pages. Turn this off to use Deep analyze instead: one AI request that reads the page and its charts, with no outside sources.

Compare buttons, shown beside some claims, need no key: one click reads the one public record the page points to (an SEC filing, a study abstract or a trial record) from that service, without the page address or your cookies. Cases you save with "Add to a case" stay in this browser only.

About the taxonomy

Subtext checks each page against a master list of persuasion and manipulation techniques, grouped into thirteen categories. Each finding names the technique, the cognitive mechanism it exploits ("why it works on you"), the evidence on the page, and a counter-move.

Three layers produce findings: heuristics (regex and DOM rules, run locally on every page), dataset lookups (outlet ownership and funding, bundled), and optional AI deep analysis (rhetoric, framing, chart reading). The rule files live in rules/ and data/ inside the extension and are plain JSON.
